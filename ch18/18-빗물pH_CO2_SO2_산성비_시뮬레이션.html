Key terms

Henry's law: S = k_H·P; the dissolved gas concentration is proportional to the partial pressure of that gas. · ppm (parts per million): for gases, mole fraction × 10⁶; partial pressure = mole fraction × total pressure. · Carbonic acid: H₂CO₃ formed from dissolved CO₂ and water; a weak acid (Ka1 4.3×10⁻⁷). · Acid rain: rain whose pH is lowered by sulfuric and nitric acids formed from SO₂ and NOₓ. · Charge balance: total positive charge = total negative charge in the solution.

Rainwater is in equilibrium with CO₂ (open system), so [CO₂(aq)] = k_H·P_CO₂ is fixed. The pH is found by solving the charge balance [H⁺] = [HCO₃⁻] + 2[CO₃²⁻] + [OH⁻] + (strong-acid anion charge) without approximation. The first step of H₂SO₄ ionizes completely; the second step is calculated with Ka2. Total pressure 101.3 kPa, 25 °C.

Clean rainwater has a pH of about 5.6

At CO₂ 400 ppm, P_CO₂ = 0.0405 kPa and the dissolved CO₂ is 1.38×10⁻⁵ M. Carbonic acid is a weak acid, so [H⁺] ≈ √(Ka1·[CO₂(aq)]) = 2.4×10⁻⁶ M, pH 5.61. Doubling CO₂ makes [H⁺] only √2 times (about 1.4 times) larger, so the pH drops by only 0.15. The amount dissolved is proportional to pressure, but the fraction that ionizes becomes smaller.

[H⁺]² ≈ Ka1·k_H·P_CO₂ (Kw neglected)

Acid rain has a pH of about 4 · strong-acid share

5.0×10⁻⁵ M H₂SO₄ releases about 1.0×10⁻⁴ M H⁺ and lowers the pH to 4.0 (a drop of 1.6 units). With more strong acid, [H⁺] rises and suppresses the ionization of carbonic acid (common-ion effect), so the carbonic-acid share becomes almost 0 and most of the [H⁺] comes from the strong acid. At the same molarity, H₂SO₄ releases about twice as much H⁺ as HNO₃. In the same way, soils with few basic ions are acidified more easily by acid rain.

[H⁺] = [HCO₃⁻] + 2[CO₃²⁻] + [OH⁻] + [HSO₄⁻] + 2[SO₄²⁻]
Limits Limits of this model

The model uses 25 °C, a total pressure of 101.3 kPa and activity coefficients of 1 (dilute solution). Real rainwater contains cations such as NH₄⁺ and Ca²⁺ and organic acids, which can change the pH. At lower temperature more CO₂ dissolves (temperature dependence is not in this model). Constants are reference values (k_H 3.4×10⁻⁴ mol L⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ (= 3.4×10⁻⁴ mol/m³·Pa), H₂CO₃ Ka1 4.3×10⁻⁷ and Ka2 5.6×10⁻¹¹, HSO₄⁻ Ka2 1.2×10⁻²). Compared with literature values, Ka1 is 4.5×10⁻⁷ (3% difference); H₂CO₃ Ka2 4.7×10⁻¹¹ and HSO₄⁻ Ka2 1.0×10⁻² differ by more than 5%, but the calculated pH in this range agrees within 0.01.

Teacher notes — open the lesson plan

Lesson plan · 5E phase and time

  • Explore and Explain, 20–25 min. Right after the topic of SO₂ and acid rain; a link activity that reviews Henry's law and the pH of weak acids. Whole-class demonstration (?mode=teacher) or individual work.

Demonstration in 3 steps

  • ① No strong acid, CO₂ 280 → 400 → 800 ppm: pH 5.69 → 5.61 → 5.46. Dissolved CO₂ doubles, but the pH changes by only 0.15.
  • ② At CO₂ 400 ppm, H₂SO₄ from 10⁻⁶ → 5×10⁻⁵ M: pH 5.4 → 4.0, strong-acid share above 99%.
  • ③ Switch to HNO₃ at the same concentration: it releases only half as much H⁺, so the pH is about 0.3 higher.

Three key questions

  • "Dissolved CO₂ doubles. Why does [H⁺] not double?"
  • "Why does carbonic acid release almost no H⁺ when there is a lot of strong acid?"
  • "To reduce acid rain, which should be reduced first: CO₂ or SO₂?"

Common misconceptions

  • MC-AR-01 "clean rainwater has pH 7" → confuses pure water with water in equilibrium with air. With no strong acid, even at CO₂ 280 ppm the pH stays near 5.7; show this to correct it.
  • MC-AR-02 "the main cause of acid rain is CO₂" → confuses weak and strong acids. Compare the ΔpH of the two prediction cases side by side (0.15 vs 1.6).

Calculation method (E2 exact equations)

  • [CO₂(aq)] = S = k_H·(ppm × 10⁻⁶)·101.325 kPa (open system). The charge balance h = (Ka1·S + Kw)/h + 2Ka1Ka2·S/h² + A(h), with A = C(h + 2Ks)/(h + Ks) for H₂SO₄ (Ks = 1.2×10⁻²) or C for HNO₃, is solved without approximation by bisection in log space (200 steps). The source split is each term on the right side of the charge balance divided by h.

Reference values and sources

  • Acid rain: unpolluted rain pH about 5.6, acid rain about pH 4, SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄. The exact solution gives 5.61 at 400 ppm and 4.00 with 5.0×10⁻⁵ M H₂SO₄, in agreement.
  • The Henry's law constant of CO₂, 3.4×10⁻⁴ mol m⁻³ Pa⁻¹ (= 3.4×10⁻⁴ mol L⁻¹ kPa⁻¹ (= 3.4×10⁻⁴ mol/m³·Pa)), is used as the reference. Some worked examples imply 3.65×10⁻⁴ (7% higher); this simulation uses the single value 3.4×10⁻⁴ throughout.
  • H₂CO₃ Ka1 4.3×10⁻⁷, Ka2 5.6×10⁻¹¹; H₂SO₄ Ka2 1.2×10⁻².
