KBS Lab · General Chemistry Simulations · Student guide

Learn general chemistry by predicting, trying and checking yourself

This English pilot has 10 simulations for IPB University students. The topics connect chemistry with agriculture and soil: fertilizers, irrigation water, soil pH, buffers and acid rain. First you predict the result, then you change the conditions yourself, then you check your understanding with short questions.

  • No login
  • Free
  • Works without internet
  • Your record stays in your browser
Go to the simulation list

1. Getting started

  1. Open the simulation list (the hub). If this is your first time, start with the cards under New here? Start with these.
  2. Choose a card to open a simulation. Each card shows the chapter, the engine type, the estimated time, the learning objective and the link to agriculture or soil.
  3. Inside a simulation, work through the steps below. The progress bar shows how far you are.
  1. Predict — Before the experiment, choose what you think will happen. A wrong guess is fine.
  2. Observe — Change the conditions with sliders and buttons. Watch the graphs and numbers.
  3. Check — Answer the questions and the fill-in-the-blank item. A wrong answer gives you an explanation.
  4. Reflect — Write what you learned in your own words, with a reason ("because …", "if …, then …").
  5. Complete — When every step is passed, the progress bar reaches 100%.

Tip. The experiment and the explanations open only after you lock in a prediction. Each simulation has three levels: Basic, Standard and Advanced. Try Advanced after you finish Standard.

2. Finding a simulation

  • Search: type a topic or a concept, for example pH, buffer, solubility or fertilizer.
  • Chapter and engine: narrow the list by chapter, or by engine type: Particles (E1), Equations (E2), Data and diagrams (E3). Press Clear to see all 10 again.
  • Continue: the simulation you opened last and the next unfinished one appear at the top of the hub.

3. Saving and backing up your record

Your predictions, answers, reflections and completions are saved automatically in the browser you are using. Another device or another browser cannot see them.

Careful. If you clear your browser data (cookies and site data) or change device, the record is lost. Save a backup file when you finish a session.

  1. On the hub, open My learning record (backup).
  2. Press Save backup file. A file named genchem_en_brain_date.json is downloaded.
  3. On another device, press Load backup file and choose that file. The records are merged.

To print a worksheet for a simulation, use the print button inside the simulation or Ctrl+P. The worksheet is designed for black-and-white printing.

4. Using it without internet

  • The whole pilot is one folder (en). Copy the folder, for example from a USB stick, to your computer.
  • Open index.html in the folder by double-clicking it. Chrome or Edge is recommended. Nothing is installed.
  • Keep the folder structure as it is; the simulations are inside the chapter folders (ch03, ch13 …).
  • On a phone or tablet, use the web address given by your lecturer.

6. Privacy

Safe to use. Your learning record is not sent to any server. No name or e-mail is collected. The record exists only in this browser and in backup files you save yourself.

  • On a shared computer (lab or library), save your backup file to your USB stick or your own cloud when you finish, and use a guest window if possible.

7. Frequently asked questions

I opened it again and my progress is 0.

You probably used another browser, or the browser data was cleared. Load your backup file with Load backup file on the hub.

It does not reach 100%.

Look at the progress bar to find the missing step (predict, observe, check or reflect). You must answer all check questions correctly and submit your reflection.

The prediction button does not work.

First choose one option, then press Lock in prediction.

My reflection is not accepted.

Write one full sentence (about 40 characters or more) that uses a key term and a reason word such as because, so, therefore or if … then.

It still does not work.

Take a screenshot and show it to your lecturer.

Remember. A wrong prediction is not a penalty; it is part of learning. Predict honestly, then look again at what surprised you.
