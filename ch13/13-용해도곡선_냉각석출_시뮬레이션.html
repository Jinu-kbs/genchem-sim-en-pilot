Key summary Key summary · KEY IDEAS
  1. Solubility is the maximum mass of solute that can dissolve in 100 g of water at a given temperature.
  2. Comparing the mass dissolved with the solubility at that temperature tells you whether the solution is unsaturated, saturated or supersaturated.
  3. The mass of crystals formed on cooling is set by the difference between the mass first dissolved and the solubility at the lower temperature.

Limits Data sources and limits of the model

The curve values are digitized from a reference solubility-curve graph; the reading uncertainty is about ±1 g. The literature value at the same temperature (CRC Handbook 91st ed., 2010, pp. 8-112 to 8-117, mass percent converted to g per 100 g of water) is shown in the readout, and differences above 5% are marked. Ranges above 5%: K₂Cr₂O₇ at 20 ℃ and above (reference curve 12–19% lower; 0.9 g higher at 0 ℃), KNO₃ at 10–20 ℃ (8–9% lower), Pb(NO₃)₂ at 0 ℃ (8% lower), NaCl at 0 ℃ and KCl at 0–30 ℃ (5–6% lower), KClO₃ at 10–20 ℃ (6–8% lower, under 1 g). Ce₂(SO₄)₃ is not in the CRC 91st ed. table (pp. 8-112 to 8-117; the only cerium compound listed is Ce(NO₃)₃, rechecked 2026-10-02), so only the reference curve is used and it is not used for quantitative grading. The crystal mass assumes that crystals form until equilibrium; in reality a supersaturated solution can persist for a while. Crystal masses are for the anhydrous salt. When crystals take water of crystallization with them, as for CaCl₂ (CaCl₂·6H₂O below 30 ℃) or Ce₂(SO₄)₃ (hydrates), the real crystal mass is larger and less water remains.

Key terms

Solubility: the maximum mass (g) of solute that can dissolve in 100 g of solvent at a given temperature. · Saturated solution: a solution that holds as much solute as the solubility allows; no more dissolves. · Unsaturated solution: a solution that holds less solute than the solubility. · Supersaturated solution: an unstable solution that holds more solute than the solubility. · Crystallization · recrystallization: dissolved solute coming out as a solid, and the purification method based on it.

Temperature and the solubility of ionic solids

For most compounds in the figure the solubility increases with temperature, but for Ce₂(SO₄)₃ it decreases. In a saturated solution, the temperature dependence of solubility is linked to the heat taken in or given out on dissolving (Le Chatelier). If dissolving is endothermic, more dissolves on heating; when ion hydration releases a lot of heat, as for Ce₂(SO₄)₃, less dissolves on heating. The NaCl curve is almost flat, so little crystallizes on cooling. Fertilizer salts such as KNO₃ show why a concentrated fertilizer solution can form crystals in a cold tank.

crystals formed = mass dissolved − S(lower temperature)

Meaning of points above and below the curve

A point below the curve is unsaturated (more can dissolve); a point on the curve (within 0.5 g) is saturated; a point above the curve holds more than can dissolve and is supersaturated. A seed crystal or a shock makes the extra amount crystallize, and the solution returns to saturation. This simulation calculates that equilibrium crystal mass. A substance with a large solubility difference (KNO₃) suits recrystallization by cooling; NaCl, with a small difference, is usually obtained by evaporation.

saturation temperature: the temperature at which S(T) = mass dissolved

Lesson plan · 5E phase and time

  • Explore · Elaborate stage, 20 min. Right after the topic "temperature effects on solubility". Whole-class demo (?mode=teacher) or individual work.

Demo in 3 steps

  • (1) Dissolve 80 g KNO₃ at 50 ℃ and cool to 20 ℃ — match the crystal mass in the readout with the vertical gap to the curve.
  • (2) Do the same with NaCl — explain the small crystal mass with the slope.
  • (3) Heat a saturated Ce₂(SO₄)₃ solution — crystals form the moment the point crosses the falling curve.

3 key questions

  • "What kind of solution is a point above the curve, in reality?"
  • "What does the curve look like for a substance that is good for recrystallization?"
  • "If a substance forms crystals when heated, what can you say about the heat of its dissolving process?"

Common misconceptions

  • MC-sol-01 "The solubility of every solid increases with temperature, without exception" → counterexamples Ce₂(SO₄)₃ and Li₂SO₄.
  • MC-sol-02 "Crystal mass = solubility at the lower temperature" → the crystal mass is the difference from the mass dissolved.
