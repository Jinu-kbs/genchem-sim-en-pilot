Ion-Product of Water Kw and pH

Water ionizes by itself (autoionization, 2H₂O ⇌ H₃O⁺ + OH⁻). If you add an acid or a base and change [H⁺], what happens to [OH⁻]? If you heat pure water, what is its pH? Every value is calculated by solving exactly the charge balance and Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] (no approximation).

Estimated time 15 min · Prerequisites: equilibrium-constant expressions, Brønsted–Lowry acids and bases

Key terms

Autoionization (self-ionization) of water: water molecules pass a proton between them, forming H₃O⁺ and OH⁻. · Ion-product constant Kw: Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻], 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ at 25 °C. · Neutral: [H⁺] = [OH⁻]. Acidic means [H⁺] > [OH⁻]; basic means [OH⁻] > [H⁺]. · pH = −log[H⁺] (common, base-10 logarithm).

Seesaw: the product is constant

Kw is an equilibrium constant, so it is constant at a given temperature. If HCl raises [H⁺] 10 times, the equilibrium shifts to the left and [OH⁻] becomes 1/10. Even in 0.010 M HCl, OH⁻ is present at 1.0×10⁻¹² M — it is not zero. Soil and irrigation-water pH are measured on this same scale.

Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ (25 °C)

Temperature: the neutral point moves

Autoionization is an endothermic reaction, so Kw increases with temperature (Le Chatelier). In pure water it is always true that [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = √Kw, so neutral pH = ½pKw. At 37 °C this is about 6.8, but the water is still neutral.

Neutral: [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = √Kw, pH = ½ pKw

Teacher notes — open the lesson plan

Lesson plan · 5E phase and time

  • Explore · Explain stage, 15 min. Works right after autoionization of water, before or after the pH scale is introduced.

Demo in 3 steps

  • (1) Water at 25 °C → HCl 0.010 M: [OH⁻]=1.0×10⁻¹² M (not zero).
  • (2) Raise the HCl concentration 10 times at each step and watch [OH⁻] fall to 1/10 each time (seesaw; the product does not change).
  • (3) Go back to pure water and change the temperature 0→60 °C: neutral pH 7.47→6.51, yet [H⁺]=[OH⁻].

3 key questions

  • "Is there no OH⁻ at all in an acidic solution?"
  • "If [H⁺] goes up 100 times, what happens to [OH⁻]?"
  • "Can we call water with pH 6.8 acidic? What was the definition of neutral?"

Common misconceptions

  • MC-kw-01: "An acidic solution has no OH⁻" → Kw is constant, so [OH⁻]=Kw/[H⁺] > 0.
  • MC-kw-02: "Water at 37 °C has pH 6.8, so it is acidic" → neutral is defined as [H⁺]=[OH⁻]; neutral pH = ½pKw(T).

Sources of values · cautions (check by PI)

  • Reference values: Kw = 1.0×10⁻¹⁴ (25 °C), Kw = 2.1×10⁻¹⁴ at 37 °C (a value often given in exercises), D₂O 8.9×10⁻¹⁶.
  • Two values for Kw at 37 °C: 2.1×10⁻¹⁴ and 2.4×10⁻¹⁴ are both found in teaching materials. The literature value (Harned–Robinson, CRC) is 2.4×10⁻¹⁴ (pKw 13.62), and the Kw(T) curve in this simulation also gives 2.40×10⁻¹⁴. Advanced Q3 uses 2.1×10⁻¹⁴ as given data — point out in class why the two values differ.
  • Kw(T) curve: Harned & Robinson (1940) interpolation log Kw = −4470.99/T + 6.0875 − 0.01706T (T in K, 0–60 °C). 25 °C 1.01×10⁻¹⁴ (shown as 1.0×10⁻¹⁴, 2 significant figures), 37 °C 2.40×10⁻¹⁴, 60 °C 9.6×10⁻¹⁴. Agrees with the CRC Handbook ion-product table.
  • Difference: 2.1×10⁻¹⁴ at 37 °C (neutral pH 6.84) is smaller than the literature 2.4×10⁻¹⁴ (6.81). Both give "about 6.8", so the learning conclusion is the same. Advanced Q3 gives the reference value as problem data.
